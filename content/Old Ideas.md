# Original Nynir Idea
> There was once a powerful pirate crew. Its captain liked to collect ships, and no, not the tiny ones in battles. Yes, they liked to collect actual, and very much gigantic, ships. Every time a pillage was successful, their ship collection grew. The population of the crew grew as well, and soon, with a fleet of 30+ ships, a whole city came of the small group of pirates. Each ship had a bustling populace, including laws, designated markets, residential areas, and production facilities that they're able to use while on the seas. Sometimes they have to go go on land to replenish their supplies, but they're not very kind to landlubbers. However, the captain forbids any harm to come to any of said landlubbers, and conducts any transactions with kingdoms in a business-like manner.
> The thing is, the city only allows two ships to come ashore, and that's the main ship where the captain is housed and another ship to carry the supplies. The rest of the fleet stays behind, floating in the middle of the sea as they waited for their captain to return. Meanwhile, they drop their anchors and throw over rope bridges onto the other ships to connect them. This allows residents of one ship to come onto the other easily. Once the main ship is back, they celebrate with a festival, and a parade is held, showcasing all of the supplies they acquired, and putting them away in a supply ship. The same parade, without the festival, is held every time they pillage successfully. Then, the fleet stops for however long the captain wants. If they roll up the bridges and raise the anchors, well, it's time to sail again.

> The early population of the city came from various places and cultures, so at first, they all just worshipped different gods. However, as time for the city went by, some of the people's gods were forgotten in favor for a whole other god that was much more important to them; the god of the sea. Now, some of these cultures were polytheistic, and thus already had their own name for the sea god, so there's no official name for them. They either refer to the god as the name they're most familiar with, or simply, "the god of our seas". The way the city pays their god respects is by giving them a small amount of their supplies as a kind of sacrifice. This god is the one they ask forgiveness from during storms, and for protection when they pass over areas where sirens or mermaids frequented.
> The city throws their dead overboard as well, and leave their souls to the flow of the sea. A dead captain is the only exception. During a dark, foggy night, they take one of the smaller boats, and place their captain's corpse in it, along with tons of dynamite, bombs, plus any crew members that had sworn absolute loyalty to the captain. They would light two lanterns. One to place on the boat, and one to stay on the ship. Then, a crew member rows the boat away from the ships, until only a small light could be seen of the other lantern. This should be true for the people left on the ship as well. If the captain had no crew members on the boat, then they would put out a second boat. One of the city's men would volunteer to row the captain, along with a small group of people on the second boat. When they get far enough, the volunteer comes back to the city on the second boat, leaving the captain far away. Now, the people on the captain's boat that are alive are not the ones that get to set off the explosives. Instead, the closest ship will fire a cannon at them, and the boat will explode or go up in flames, meaning that the captain has truly left. The next seven days in the city are left ungoverned for the population to mourn.
> A captain will always serve until their death. A captain with no such intention is not meant to be captain, and a mutiny would be arranged. Though, none have happened so far. The next captain is decided while the current captain is still alive. It's usually one of the captain's own offspring, but there have been cases where someone completely unrelated was chosen. That chosen person would then be known as the captain's apprentice. If you are chosen, you must not say a word to anybody, including your immediate family. This apprentice could change at any time if the captain deems them unfit or useless, who are then sworn to secrecy or death. I've said before that after a captain dies, the week after their funeral is left with no captain in order for the whole city to mourn, but really it's for the apprentice to prepare their rise to captaincy. During these days, the apprentice is choosing their crew, planning their speech, their first pillage, and getting their mark as captain (which is a brand that's to be burned on the apprentice's skin). As soon as these seven days are up, the captain is introduced to the rest of the city in a ceremony that is only held by the captain himself using his own connections.

# ?? funeral rites
> Whenever someone in a family dies, they take the body to be cremated. The corpse is burned until it's only ashes, then it is taken to the candlemaker. The maker mixes this person's ashes with the wax, imbuing it with the person's soul and spirit. The person is given the chance to decide the look and design of their candle while they're still alive, but if there was none, the family would decide, though there is a default design to every candle if the family doesn't want to decide for them. After the candle has been made, it is given back to the family, and they place it in their family candlehouse. This is when the actual funeral happens. The candle is lit, and the spirit of the person rises from the smoke. The divide between the living and the dead is broken for a moment inside this candlehouse, and the family is able to communicate with their departed person's soul.
> The funeral consists of three parts. The initial lighting ceremony, talking to the departed and noting their afterlife wishes, and the putting out of the fire. The lighting and blowing cannot be done by someone that the spirit did not like, or else the spirit won't come. This person can be indicated in the departed's will, so if it's not, good luck. After the funeral, the family has a month to finish all of the departed's wishes or unfinished business. If there's still work left after a month, it's considered disgraceful and very much frowned upon by society.
> The departed's candle and their spirit will stay in the family candlehouse. After the initial funeral, anyone can light the candle and call upon the person's spirit, allowing them to talk whenever. Though, the barrier only allows one spirit per candlehouse at a time. The family is able to communicate with their dead loved ones until the candle is gone. If a candle is broken in some way, perhaps by a typhoon or a storm, it's considered bad luck, both for the family and the spirit of the candle, which is why candlehouses are treated carefully and placed in safe locations.


# imperial tournament

You need to take an intelligence test before you're able to go on to the other contests. It's made by various professors and advisors in multiple subjects, and it's incredibly difficult. But, just passing the test isn't enough. You have to be in the top 14 scorers to be able to join the competition itself.
After that, the main event begins. The tournament is held at the arena in the span of seven days. Each day, the participants compete against each other in a contest designed by the ministers to determine the qualities of a ruler.
The last part of it is a fighting contest. Two contestants are pit against each other, where the winner fights the other winner. While not all that important in the choosing, this is the most anticipated part of the tournament. It's done during the seventh day, where you'll find the whole arena to be packed with people.
The ministers then look over each of their standings in every contest, including their test scores and where they made mistakes. When they've decided, the head minister announces the name at another event they call the Imperial Announcement.
