You would need to finish Combat School and some minor courses in the School of Law for the highest chance of getting hired. Another example is if you want to be a bard. You would finish either School of Arts or Magic (whichever you want to focus on), and take minor courses in the other school respectively. There are various Standard Schools scattered throughout the continent, generally one or two for each kingdom. The other schools are much rarer. Magic and Arts have two schools, while Combat only has one school. They are located near large cities/kingdoms, and the students need to move there or travel multiple times to be able to attend.

Standard schools don't need much to let you in. Just provide proper credentials and a letter from your government allowing you or your child to go. For advanced learning in standard or in any specialty school, you need those same credentials again, pass an exam, proof that you finished standard learning, and a minimum of ten gold coins for specialty, and five for advanced standard. A certificate that you also finished Advanced Standard is optional.

You can have a major school, and minor courses from other schools. This includes learning a second language from advanced standard, simple spells from magic, self defense from combat, others from arts, etc etc. You would need to apply to the school of the minor course and pay a minimum of fifty silvers. You can do them during school breaks.

Schooling starts at five years old, and can take any number of years according to the school you're going to. You can take up to ten minor courses for each school whenever you want, but you can only have one major school.

Standard Learning is necessary. There is still the option of hiring tutors or teaching the child yourself if you feel that that's better, but sending them to a proper establishment can help them have a more focused education. The next stage is more optional. You can do it at a later age, but there are some stigmas around doing it like that.

These schools have their own staff of teachers, but may sometimes call on professionals/alumni to help teach/demonstrate.

## Standard School

There's at least one standard school in every country. Children attend this from five to ten years of age. It teaches them everything, from language to farming to simple self defense. Simple arithmetic, spoken and written language, courtesy, and simple history are necessary to learn, but other than those, the student or their parents get to choose which other classes to take.

After finishing Standard School, the student can advance to the other schools of specialty if they want to do something specific, or stay at the school for Advanced Standard, which means five more years. You are still able to apply to other schools when you finish Advanced Standard if you wish.

Other schools do not provide language, and as long as there is a teacher, any language is taught here. Parenthood is also taught here, including agriculture and simple trade. They could also teach you more arithmetic, but for really advanced learning, you must go to a specialty school. Common jobs like hunters, fishermen, or farmers only really need standard learning.

## Specialty Schools

### School of Magic

There are two (2) schools of magic in the world.

**Tomora Academy**

Tomora Academy is situated at the top of Mount Highclaw. Even though it was already thousands of feet above ground, the school is made up of numerous towers and spires that went much higher. Due to the exterior's cold climate, all rooms inside are always warmed with the breaths of a captured white dragon that they keep in the basement. The walls and floor are made of a purple variety of quartz, and its classrooms are state-of-the-art. Only the best of the best (or in most cases, the richest of the rich) students are able to attend this prestigious magic school.

**Louria's School of the Arcane**

LSA stands near the south of the world, with an open air campus and widespread buildings. Its crest features the sun as well as a griffin in the middle. This is because the school has been taking care of the same family of griffins for centuries now. They've become somewhat of a tourist attraction and a source of extra money should the school need it. This griffin family gets passed down to every generation of the Lourias, who own the school. The LSA accepts everyone, and they believe every student has the capabilities of performing magic.

The two schools have a bitter rivalry with the other. Especially during Trial Week.

### School of Combat

The only School of Combat in Oros is currently run by a bronze dragon for who knows how long. The dragon usually takes the form of a male human and seems to prefer it over his other scaly self.

The school stands on an uninhabited island in the middle of a great forest. It is the only one without an entrance test, since the journey there is already quite risky and dangerous that making it to the school is enough to accept you. Plus, they really shouldn't send kids back out there on a return trip.

Additionally, it is also the only one without a name. There used to be a name for it a long time ago, but when the dragon took over from the last dean, nobody's been able to remember it.

### School of Arts

Like magic, there are also two schools of arts, except this time, they both teach different things from each other. In actuality, the school of arts is divided into three strands. The fine arts (like music, theatre, painting, and others), business and law, and then the sciences.

#### Trial Week

Every beginning of an Honor's Year, the students of each school meet in the country of Alfae for a tournament. The tournament is only for fun, and allows the kids a chance to mingle with students from other schools. They will take part in various activities, with handicaps provided to those who need them. The school that wins will be the priority of the whole year's shipments.

Trial Week, assuming everything is right on schedule (which it always is), always starts with the passing of a comet and ends with a double-moon eclipse.

**(Free) Standard Learning**

- children five to ten years of age at standard school.

**(5g) Advanced Standard**

- standard school again, but with more details. For older children. Lasts for three more years.

**(10g) Specialty Education**

- specialty school or major school of the student.

**(50s) Minor Courses**

- a course a student can take from a different school of choice. Done during school breaks. Each course only needs a certain amount of time so you're able to take multiple courses in one break, and you can continue to self study if you'd like to.

**(10s) Testing**

- a service that schools provide for people that are not their own students. For example, you've been studying a foreign language on your own. You can pay a standard school for an exam in that language to test your skills. Another example is if you only learned martial arts from your father, and want to see how effective it is to the combat school attendants.
